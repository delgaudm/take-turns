A small manifesto

The phone is not invited.

Okay, one phone.

The internet put everyone in constant contact and somehow made it harder to be together. Take Turns is a small refusal: the phone sets up the fun, then gets out of the way.

Your attention is not inventory

Most free apps are not free. You pay with your habits, location, relationships, attention, and an unnervingly detailed record of every moment your thumb hesitated.

We do not want any of that.

Take Turns has no accounts, advertising, analytics, tracking pixels, engagement notifications, behavioral profiles, or data-broker side quests. We do not know who you are.

Honestly, it’s refreshing.

The room is the network

There are no multiplayer lobbies because the other players are sitting next to you.

There are no friend requests because presumably you handled that part already.

There are no daily streaks, virtual currencies, loot boxes, leaderboards, or notifications announcing that your friends “miss you.” If your friends miss you, they can say so with their human mouths.

Offline is a feature

Install Take Turns once and the games work without a connection. That is useful on road trips, at campsites, in waiting rooms, and anywhere the Wi-Fi password is written in illegible cursive behind the bar.

It is also a boundary. A game that does not need a server has fewer opportunities to spy on you, manipulate you, disappear after an acquisition, or suddenly develop a Premium Gold Family Plus plan.

Free means there is no trapdoor

These games are not a demo.

There is no upgrade waiting behind the fun parts. No seven-day trial. No introductory price. No venture-backed strategy to establish dominance in the lucrative “people speaking to their families” market.

They are given away because gathering around something silly is good.

Not every enjoyable thing needs to become an industry.

The phone works for the group

Technology is excellent at shuffling cards, keeping time, remembering scores, and producing a tiny gavel sound.

People are better at bluffing, arguing, guessing, storytelling, laughing, and remembering the night afterward.

We intend to preserve that division of labor.

Our promise

  • People over profiles
  • Conversation over engagement
  • Local storage over surveillance
  • Useful constraints over endless features
  • Shared laughter over individual scrolling
  • A good evening over “time in app”

The phone gets one turn.Then it’s yours.

Go play. Take turns. Have fun.

# Take Turns Design Philosophy

Take Turns is a collection of offline games for people who are physically
together. The phone is a shared game board, card deck, timer, or scorekeeper.
It supports play; it is not the place where play happens.

## The Experience

Take Turns games are conversational and social. Think card games and parlor
games, not beer pong.

They are meant to replace the awkward silence when a group is stuck somewhere
together:

- On a road trip
- At a campsite
- In a waiting room
- Around a dinner table
- At a small party
- On the couch after Thanksgiving dinner

The best moments happen when players look away from the phone and interact with
one another.

## Product Constraints

Every game must:

- Work with one shared phone and no other required props
- Be playable entirely in person
- Work without an internet connection after installation or the initial visit
- Require no accounts, game lobby, second screen, or remote players
- Keep all game state on the device
- Be family-friendly
- Accommodate ordinary conversation volume and a relaxed room
- Explain itself quickly enough that a group can begin without studying rules
- Use large, clear controls that are comfortable when passing a phone around
- Remain useful when opened directly as a self-contained HTML page

Paper may be suggested for a game when it substantially improves play, but the
core collection should favor games that need only the shared phone.

## Design Character

Games should feel:

- Conversational rather than rowdy
- Social rather than screen-focused
- Approachable rather than performative
- Playful without embarrassing players
- Structured enough to remove uncertainty
- Flexible enough for friends, couples, and families

Avoid mechanics that depend on shouting, physical stunts, drinking, public
performance, fast device handling, or everyone watching a screen continuously.

Competition is welcome, but it should create conversation, suspense, cleverness,
or laughter rather than aggression.

## The Phone's Role

The phone may:

- Reveal a private word or role to one player at a time
- Deal prompts, questions, categories, or challenges
- Track turns, rounds, and scores
- Run a timer or provide a subtle audio cue
- Preserve seen-card history or optional saved cards
- Explain the rules and guide handoffs

The phone should not:

- Simulate a multiplayer lobby
- Require each player to enter actions that can happen naturally in the room
- Replace spoken conversation with typing
- Use speech recognition to referee ordinary play
- Demand constant attention between turns
- Add complexity simply because software makes it possible

When the group can handle something naturally—speaking a clue, deciding whether
an answer is correct, or resolving a friendly rules dispute—the group should
handle it.

## A Good Take Turns Game

A strong game can usually be summarized in a few sentences:

1. The phone presents or privately reveals something.
2. Players talk, think, guess, debate, or create together.
3. The phone advances the game and remembers only what is useful.

Before adding a feature, ask:

> Does this create a better moment between the people in the room, or does it
> merely create more interaction with the phone?

Prefer the first.
